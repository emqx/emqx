The `mqtt` connector health check now verifies the connection to the remote broker end to end: it sends an MQTT `PINGREQ` and waits for the `PINGRESP`, bounded by half of the configured `resource_opts.health_check_timeout`, instead of only inspecting the local client state.  A remote broker that keeps the TCP connection open while packets are dropped is reported as `disconnected` as soon as the probe goes unanswered, so the resource is recreated and the action's buffer workers keep the messages queued instead of handing them to a connection that cannot deliver them; the client's own keepalive is no longer what notices the silence (1 to 2 x `keepalive`, 160 s by default).

Connector status reasons no longer explain a connection closed by the peer as a listener capacity problem.
